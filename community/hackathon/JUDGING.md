# AeglysAI judging protocol

Round 1 shortlists submissions; Round 2 ranks finalists. Do not combine round scores.

| Round 1 criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Functionality | 30 |
| AI/ML or intelligent feature | 25 |
| Relevance and creativity | 20 |
| Reproducibility | 15 |
| Video clarity | 10 |

| Round 2 criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Live demonstration | 35 |
| Technical understanding | 25 |
| Solution quality and safety | 20 |
| Technical Q&A | 15 |
| Presentation | 5 |

Propose 5–10 finalist teams, subject to eligible submissions and organizer capacity. This is a planning range, not an asserted finalist count or guaranteed selection. Publish capacity and eligibility rules before opening the event.

Use equally weighted independent judges and the arithmetic mean within each round. Round 1 ties use functionality, then AI/ML or intelligent feature, then reproducibility. Round 2 ties use live demonstration, then technical understanding, then solution quality and safety. Remaining ties receive the same additional demonstration/Q&A opportunity and independent panel review; if still equal, record a shared rank and explain any capacity decision under published rules. Do not invent a secret tie-break after seeing scores.

Judges disclose team membership, close personal/financial relationships, supervision and mentoring conflicts privately to the organizer. Recuse conflicted judges from that team; assign unconflicted replacements so teams receive equivalent review. Record assignments, rubrics, scores, reasons and corrections privately. Do not publish individual judging scores or private comments. Public finalists, winners and showcases require approved disclosure.


## Originality, assistance and technical clarification

Round 1 is code and video technical evaluation. Round 2 finalists demonstrate live over Google Meet and answer judges' technical questions. Qualification eligibility is separate from competition scoring.

Original work must be distinguished from pre-existing code, libraries, datasets and models. Cite sources, comply with licenses and identify the team's implemented contribution; do not represent copied work as original. AI assistance is permitted when disclosed with the tool, scope and human verification; never upload confidential or personal records to an assistant. Teams remain responsible for correctness, safety and explaining their code. Pre-existing libraries and frameworks are allowed with attribution and license review. Meaningful intelligent functionality may use a justified rule/statistical method; merely naming AI without executable behavior is insufficient.

Judges may request reproducibility or technical clarification using equivalent time and scope for similarly situated teams. Log clarification privately; do not permit undisclosed post-deadline feature development. Apply published correction/disqualification rules consistently for misrepresentation, unsafe artifacts or inaccessible evidence, allowing a documented response before final decisions. Event-specific deadlines and decision authority must be configured before operation.
